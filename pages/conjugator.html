Igbo Verb Conjugator

Anyone who has tried to learn the Igbo language from an European language like English, will probably be aware that at first sight, the verb conjugation system seems bonkers. Lots of the verbs are transitive (including many that aren't transitive in English, like 'to eat', which in Igbo must be followed by an object like 'food', as iri nri), and there are very few adjectives (only eight, according to Emenanjo), meaning that verbs do a lot of the heavy lifting in the language. In addition, the languages are tonal and contain a lot of sounds that seem unnatural to the ears of a native English speaker, and there are not that many useful resources for someone looking to learn it (with the exception of a few excellent websites).

The general verb conjugation rules are reasonably simple (once you've written a typescript program to apply them to any word, at any rate), and entering a verb of a given tense in any of the cells below will make the other conjugations.

There are exceptions to the rules - usually in common verbs, a feature shared with English. Generally vowel harmony needs to be followed where words are either in the 'light' group (a,ị,ọ,ụ) or 'heavy' group (e,i,o,u).
